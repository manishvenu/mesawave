## How to work with me: I'm learning about meshes
I'm a first-year ATOC master's student who has taken over this work and I'm **new to unstructured meshes**. My goal is to understand what we're doing, not only to get it done. So please:
- Explain concepts before or while doing the work (nodes, connectivity, why triangles, how OceanMesh2D works, ESMF mesh files), and define terms the first time they come up.
- Go step by step. Do not run long pipelines or make big changes without telling me what each step does and why.
- Use analogies to what I know. I'm used to logically rectangular grids like MOM6 (4 neighbours per node, implicit (i, j) connectivity) and I'm taking an intro wave class (dispersion relation, group velocity).
- Check my understanding now and then with a short question, and correct me directly when I have something wrong.
- Say when you are unsure or haven't verified something, rather than guessing.

What I've worked through so far:
- A node is a point where the model computes, and WW3 keeps a full wave spectrum at each node. Neighbours are implicit on a MOM6 grid, but an unstructured mesh must store node coordinates and triangle connectivity explicitly.
- In a perfect equilateral-triangle lattice, every interior node has 6 neighbours. A node with 5 or 7 is a topological defect (like a crystal dislocation), and relaxing positions can't remove it.
- A mesh of triangles covering a sphere needs exactly 12 five-neighbour nodes (icosahedron). Quad meshes have the same issue, which is why the MOM6 tripole puts its singularities over land.
- ESMF has its own mesh format: nodeCoords, elementConn, and numElementConn (needed because connectivity is a flat 1D array that allows mixed element types).
- OceanMesh2D uses DistMesh (spring relaxation), and the initial points fix the point density and most of the connectivity pattern.
- I still need to learn: how DistMesh relaxation works in detail, what WW3's propagation scheme does on a triangular mesh, and how the ESMF mesh file is laid out.

## Mesh summary
- 1,285,928 nodes, 2,523,094 triangles. Mean element quality 0.992. Spacing within 1% of target (median) in every zone.
- Size function: h = min(25, 10 + 0.15 d_coast, 12 + 0.15 d_TC, 5 + 0.15 max(d_US - 100, 0)) km.
- ~7% over the 1.2M-node proposal budget, ~23% cheaper than wu025r004 (1.67M).
- Generated by OceanMesh2D (DistMesh spring relaxation) from our own initial points: one equilateral lattice per size level, in latitude rows, each row staggered by a golden-ratio longitude.

## Current problem: the "dots"
- ~5,041 open-ocean nodes (~1%) are isolated 5/7-neighbour pairs. Nearby triangles are ~1 km off the 25 km target.
- Cause: rows along latitude circles lose a point poleward, and each change in row length needs one 5/7 pair. These are topological defects (like edge dislocations), so relaxing node positions won't remove them.
- On a sphere, defects are unavoidable (an icosahedral triangle mesh needs exactly 12 five-neighbour nodes). The issue is the thousands of extra pairs.
- v1 had the pairs lined up along fixed meridians, and v2's golden-ratio stagger scattered them but did not remove them.
